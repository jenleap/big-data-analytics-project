Context — Why this question matters

Think of this project as a shopping-cart detective story: when one product vanishes, where do its shoppers go? Do they switch to a similar product, or do they stop buying the whole kit — affecting items that were bought together?

Retail reality check

Retailers constantly tidy their shelves to boost profit — adding new items, delisting slow movers, and juggling inventory. A key idea here is transferability: if product A is removed, what fraction of its sales will transfer to other products (and which ones)?

High transferability

If other products can absorb most of the lost sales, delisting is low-risk.

Complements matter

Removing a product can ripple through the basket: pairs that are bought together may lose sales too.

A more accessible approach

Classic choice models estimate diversion or demand shifts using price, promotions, and stockout records — data that’s often locked behind corporate doors. This project takes a different, practical route: we use basket-level data — the actual items customers buy together — to estimate substitution and complementarity. It’s behaviour-based, easily available for many retailers, and powerful for predicting real-world effects.

What we want to answer (the research questions)

  1. Who will pick up the slack?
    Which products are most likely to absorb sales if a particular product is removed — i.e., the likely substitutes (transfer targets).
  2. How much will they absorb?
    What percentage of a removed product’s sales will each substituting product capture — this quantifies revenue transfer and helps rank delisting risk.

How this feeds decisions

Together, these answers give retailers practical, low-friction tools to:

  • Rank delisting candidates by revenue risk.
  • Simulate the revenue impact of removing or adding items.
  • Plan cross-promotions and assortment changes that protect complementary sales.
